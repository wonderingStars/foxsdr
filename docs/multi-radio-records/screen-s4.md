## Job B: the screen on LIVE radios

WHAT WAS THERE. S3 had already put the real path in: `Workspace::readFacts` reads `fact.radio.count` and `fact.radio.<n>.state`; `select()` sends RADIO_FOCUS; each pane reads `read_radio_state` / `read_radio_spectrum`; gestures are aimed with `FoxCommand.target`. The "made-up side" is only the bounded run's `testradios=N` seam, which `main.cpp` consults when a `SeamRadios` exists. Nothing of it was replaced: the seam STAYS, for the S3 drive scripts (`workspace-*.txt`, header `testradios=N`) and `test_slint_workspace_host`, which need panes without a second engine radio; none of it is reached when the key is not set. What job B did was run the real path against the real engine and fix what that showed.

WHAT IT SHOWED (each fixed, in 233addf3 and 7289cbe6):
1. The host read the radio facts only when the engine's `listSeq` moved. The engine's list fingerprint (`engine_api_lists.cpp apiListsFingerprint`) holds no receiver count, so adding a radio does not move it (found by reading it, then by the design of the fix: the facts are now read every eighth tick as well, 0.27 s). ENGINE GAP, below.
2. A slot that emptied kept its `RadioView` (zoom, history, caches): `RadioSet::reset`.
3. A removed SELECTED radio: the engine keeps its number as the session's focus and answers NO_DEVICE to every focused read; the tick's first call is `read_state`, which returned before the code that notices the radio has gone. Found live: `live-two` never got back to one radio (3 steps red). Fixed: a NO_DEVICE read refreshes the radios from the facts and sends RADIO_FOCUS for the selected radio.

THE DRIVE VERB (test only, bounded runs): `radio add [<n> [<toneHz>]]` opens a GENERATOR radio through the real RADIO_ADD (number n, 0 = the lowest free; started running; the tone in Hz from its centre, none = 25 kHz times its number), `radio remove <n>` sends RADIO_REMOVE. Read-backs: `workspace.peakoff.<n>` / `peakdb.<n>` (where the highest bin of radio n's last frame is, in Hz from the frame's centre, and its level), `workspace.r.<n>.<field>` (running, tuned, centre, mode, volume, muted, agc, agcfixed, nb, nbthreshold, device, rate: radio n's own state), `workspace.tickms` / `tickp95` / `tickn` (the host's own tick time over 300 ticks). ## Job C: stage S4

### Decisions and deviations

1. WHERE THINGS ARE. The design (3.4) names four places and the lane brief fixed the rest:
   - THE STRIP (DEVIATION: two places). A block at the LEFT of the receiver strip, in front of today's STOP and lamps, only while `WorkspaceModel.radio-count > 1` and the window is 1760 px or more: "Controls follow" with a key per radio of the page shown, `STOP R<n>` and `STOP ALL` / `START ALL`, and the MASTER lamps (`ui/radio_strip.slint` `RadioStripBlock`, four rows, 262 px). The strip of a narrower window is full already (it needs about 1750 px at the sheet's sizes): the first draft put the block there at every width and, at 1440 x 900, pushed ANTENNA POWER / BIAS TEE out of the strip (found in the first window shot). Below 1760 px the same keys and lamps are ONE row of the 30 px bar over the receiver view (`RadioStripRow`, `WorkspaceBar.compact`). The bar exists only on the RECEIVER tab, so in a narrow window the row is not on the PATCH or SCOPE tabs. With one radio neither is instantiated and the strip is the strip it was.
   - THE SELECTOR shows the radios of the page shown: layout One shows the block of four that holds the selected radio, a multi-pane layout the block of four that holds the page's first pane (Side by side page 3 = radios 5-6 shows the keys 5 to 8). Only radios that exist get a key.
   - MASTER LAMPS: one per OPEN radio (up to eight), the lamp captioned with the radio's number, green running, red failed, dark stopped (a radio lent to the patch or being opened is dark and its tip says so in words); a click selects the radio and turns the page to it (`Workspace::select` sets the page that holds it).
   - STOP Rn stops the SELECTED radio (RUN 0 aimed at it) and is live only while that radio runs (its START is the pane's key, START ALL, or the strip's own key, which already toggles the selected radio). STOP ALL / START ALL is ONE key: it reads STOP ALL while any radio runs and START ALL while none does, and sends RUN with `FOXAPI_TARGET_ALL_RADIOS`.
   - THE AUDIO MIX is at the foot of the sections panel (above the 44 px source line), only with several radios open: per radio an R-tag, a route key that cycles both / left / right / none (RADIO_AUDIO), MUTE (SET_MUTED aimed at the radio), SOLO (RADIO_SOLO) and a volume slider (SET_VOLUME aimed at the radio). It is no taller than 200 px; with more than five radios it scrolls. The design drew the route as a readout; it is a key (the design's own note says the control goes there).
   - THE R-TAG over the sections panel header shows the selected radio, only with several radios open.
   - STATUS CHIPS: one per open radio, in front of today's chips: "R2", its state word (RUNNING, STOPPED, FAIL, OPENING, LENT TO PATCH; existing catalogue words except LENT TO PATCH, which is prepared), its frequency and mode from ITS OWN state ("--" until that radio's state has been read, never another radio's). A click selects the radio. With more than four radios, or a window under 1500 px, the chip is the tag and the lamp only and the words are its detail lines: the status bar has no room for them beside today's chips and its keys (first draft: five chips pushed "Report a bug" out of the bar).
   - THE ADD-A-RADIO CARD: in every empty cell of the shown page while a slot is free (replaces S3's quiet well; with eight radios open no cell is empty), and as a SHEET opened by a new key "Add a radio" at the right of the status bar. The card lists the devices not already held and "Look for radios again".
   - REMOVING: an icon-only close key on a pane's header for radios 2 and up (radio 1 is always there). A stopped radio goes at once; a running or recording one asks first (a sheet: "Remove Radio n?", what removal does, Remove / Cancel).
2. THE CARD'S CHOICES. The rows are FOXAPI_LIST_DEVICES minus what a second radio cannot open: the I/Q file row and the sound card row (the engine refuses them for a second radio: RADIO_ADD answers UNSUPPORTED "a second radio opens a scanned device or the signal generator"), Pluto and rtl_tcp rows (their ids are `open-pluto:` / `open-rtltcp:`, which RADIO_ADD refuses). The design's "I/Q file" and "Network (rtl_tcp)" keys are therefore NOT OFFERED: a seam for the engine lane (RADIO_ADD for a file and for an rtl_tcp address). The signal generator is always offered: every radio has a generator of its own. A scanned device is left out while an open radio holds it (compared as the key `<kind>|<args>` against `fact.radio.<n>.device`); the engine's ledger still has the last word and a refusal is shown under the keys exactly as the engine worded it (`AddOutcome`, byte for byte).
3. A RADIO ADDED FROM THE CARD STARTS RUNNING (the first radio does); when it is the first radio beside the first and the layout is One, the layout turns to Side by side so the user sees what they did. Adding does not otherwise move the selection.
4. THE ONE-RADIO USER sees exactly one new thing: a ghost key "Add a radio" in the status bar (at the right, before "Request a feature"). Nothing else is instantiated: no strip block, no tag, no mix, no chips, no bar (the `if WorkspaceModel.radio-count > 1` guards), no close key (no pane). It opens the card as a sheet; the first radio added turns the layout to Side by side.
5. SOLO IS THE HOST'S RECORD. The engine publishes no read-back of the solo radio (`fact.radio.<n>.audio` is the route only). The key's lit state is the host's own `soloRadio`, cleared when that radio goes (the engine clears its own too). A second window, or the web client, could set a solo this window does not see: a seam named in the report.
6. FACTS ARE CACHED. `fact.radio.<n>.{state,device,label,audio,refusal}` are read for every open radio when the radios are refreshed (list changes, every eighth tick, after every answer to an add, a remove or a route) - not every tick; the radios' STATES are read every tick (`read_radio_state`, one per open radio that is not the selected one) because the lamps, the mix and the chips show them.
7. KEYS AND LABELS. Every control names its radio and no label repeats: selector keys "Controls follow Radio n" (so the panes' "Select Radio n" stay unique), lamps "Radio n master", "STOP R<n>", "STOP ALL" / "START ALL", "Output of Radio n", "Mute Radio n", "Solo Radio n", "Radio n: VOLUME", pane close key "Remove Radio n". The card's keys are "Add <device>"; with several empty cells the same label repeats in each (drive scripts use `#n`); the card's group is "Add a radio to pane n", the sheet's group has none so the status bar's key is the one control called "Add a radio".


## What the user can and cannot do now

With ONE radio the window is today's, plus ONE new thing: a small ghost key "Add a radio" at the right of the status bar, before "Request a feature". Nothing else of the stage is instantiated with one radio (the `radio-count > 1` guards): no strip block, no bar, no tag, no mix, no chips, no close key (there is no pane). The status bar's chips have about 85 px less room (the key's width) and elide their detail sooner (`s4-1440-one-workbench.png`: "Signal gene..."; no pre-S4 shot was kept to compare, so how much sooner is not measured). Nothing else is added to that window. Every existing drive script, component test and screenshot comparison passes unedited (the S3 test `test_slint_radio_workspace` gained assertions for the close key; none was changed).

With several radios open, in both skins:
- ADD: the status bar's key opens a sheet; each empty cell of a multi-pane layout shows the same card for ITS slot. Both list the signal generator (a radio of its own each time) and the scanned radios no open radio holds; "Look for radios again" rescans. A radio added from the card starts running; the first radio beside the first turns the layout to Side by side. The engine's refusal (a device held by another radio or by the patch, the limit of eight open radios counting patch radios) is shown under the keys exactly as the engine words it.
- REMOVE: the close key on radios 2 and up: a stopped radio goes at once; a running or recording one asks first (Remove / Cancel). Removing the selected radio moves the selection to the lowest radio left and the engine's focus with it.
- SELECT: the strip's selector ("Controls follow" R1 to R4 of the page shown; with more than four radios it follows the page), a MASTER lamp (also turns the page to the radio), a status chip, or a click in a pane (S3). The strip, the SIGNAL bank and the panel's R-tag follow, AGC and noise blanker values included (live-agc).
- STOP: STOP Rn stops the selected radio (live only while it runs); STOP ALL stops every open radio and reads START ALL while none runs; a pane's own STOP / START (S3) acts on its radio.
- AUDIO MIX (foot of the sections panel): per radio the route (both, left, right, none), MUTE, SOLO and volume. Read back from the engine: the route (`fact.radio.<n>.audio`), mute and volume (the radio's own state). SOLO is the host's record.
- STATUS BAR: one chip per open radio (lamp, "R2", state word and figures; tag and lamp only above four radios or under 1500 px), a click selects it.
- WIDE windows (1760 px and up) carry the block in the strip; narrower windows carry it as one row in the 30 px bar over the receiver view.

CANNOT YET: open an I/Q file, an rtl_tcp address, a Pluto or the sound card as a second radio (RADIO_ADD refuses them: an engine item); link tuning, "Start together" as a toolbar key (START ALL in the strip does it), keyboard selection (Ctrl+1 to 8, page keys), a saved layout, the Runs-on picker (S5); decoders or plugins on radios 2 to 8 (engine stage 6: the pane's decoder chip is empty); a gain, antenna or sample-rate list for a radio other than the first (see seams: a trap, below); any saved setting for radios 2 to 8 (engine stage 5: after a restart only radio 1 is back). The radios' controls are clipped out of the bar in a narrow window (800 px: see "Screenshots").
